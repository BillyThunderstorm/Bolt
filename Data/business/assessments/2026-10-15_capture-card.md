# Assessment: Capture Card (4K HDMI in / 1080p60 USB 3.0, HDMI-to-USB)

- **Price paid / where bought / date:** $21.49 · Amazon · 2026-09-30
- **Category:** Gaming / Tech
- **Claims tested:**
  1. 1080p HD capture from any HDMI source (PS4, PS3, Xbox One, Xbox 360, Wii U, DVD, DSLR, camera, security camera, set-top box).
  2. Plug and play: no driver or external power; shows up as a webcam and auto-detects input/output.
  3. Works on Windows and macOS; USB 3.0 low latency for streaming to Twitch, YouTube, Facebook, Twitter, OBS, PotPlayer, VLC.
- **Test method:** Set up on Mac. Recorded, went live, and edited clips from recordings captured through the card. Plugged into PS5, Xbox, and Nintendo Switch and streamed on Twitch.
- **Duration:** 14 days / 5 uses
- **Day-by-day notes:**
  - Day 1: Simple and easy to set up.
  - Day 3: Cords are pretty short, which makes it hard to move things and set the card somewhere stable without worry.
  - Day 7: I would have saved hundreds of dollars and time if I had bought this first.
- **Measurable results:** Did everything it claimed. Still waiting to see how it holds up over long streaming days.
- **Pros:** Everything it says it does, it does. Nothing complicated. No extra software to download.
- **Cons:** Short cords. Nothing else yet, but it's only been 2 weeks.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone who needs a capture card, especially a first one.
- **Who it's NOT for:** Name-brand buyers, or anyone who wants all their gear from the same brand. It's an unknown maker (which I actually like), so beyond 'it does 4K' there's no brand story to point to.
- **Verdict + score (10/10):** Before you get pulled toward the big-name brand, buy this first. It does everything at a much lower price. Nothing was complicated or a hassle: plug it in, open OBS, pick the output, done. It honestly upset me that I bought the other one first just because it's advertised so much. I'd recommend this over Elgato 1,000 times over.
- **Disclosure:** Bought with my own money.
