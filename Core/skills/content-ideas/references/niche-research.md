# Niche Research

Strategies for finding trending topics and content gaps in gaming, tech, and creator culture.

## Sources to Check Regularly

- **YouTube search suggest**: Type a seed keyword and read the autocomplete dropdown. Free, real-time, reflects what people are actually typing.
- **Google Trends**: Compare multiple terms side by side. Good for spotting rising interest before it peaks.
- **Twitch categories and tags**: See what's climbing in viewership. Rising categories often mean fresh audiences.
- **TikTok Creative Center**: Trending hashtags and sounds in gaming/tech verticals.
- **Reddit**: r/Games, r/technology, r/Twitch, subreddits for specific games or products. Sort by rising, not hot. Rising threads are pre-viral.
- **X (Twitter)**: Follow niche creators and hashtags. Search for "[topic] review" or "[topic] tips" and sort by Latest.
- **Amazon Best Sellers**: Browse the target category. What's #1 today tells you demand; what's climbing tells you momentum.
- **Steam charts / player counts**: For games, spikes in concurrent players signal a moment worth reacting to.

## Finding Content Gaps

1. Pick three to five popular creators in your niche.
2. List the topics they cover repeatedly.
3. Note what viewers ask in their comments that nobody answers.
4. Check whether smaller creators are covering that unanswered question successfully.
5. If it's asked often but rarely answered well, that's your gap.

## Timing Rules

- **New releases**: Cover within 24 to 48 hours for maximum relevance. Speed beats polish.
- **Trends**: Enter early, exit before saturation. If five similar titles already exist, find a different angle rather than another generic take.
- **Seasonal**: Holiday shopping and year-end lists start planning two to three months early.
- **Evergreen**: Longevity tests and budget roundups don't expire. Batch them when you have downtime.

## Signals Worth Acting On

- A question appearing in multiple creators' comment sections
- Sudden drops in a popular game's player count (people want to know why)
- A product getting widespread complaints that lack independent testing
- Viewer requests that repeat across platforms
- Platform algorithm pushes favoring tutorial or comparison formats

## Anti-Patterns

- Chasing every trend equally. Choose ones that overlap your existing audience.
- Relying on one source. Cross-check at least two before committing to a topic.
- Ignoring negative feedback. Viewer complaints are often better idea seeds than praise.
